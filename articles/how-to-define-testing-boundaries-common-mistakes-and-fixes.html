How to define testing boundaries: common mistakes and fixes

Why this matters

Testing boundaries determine what the assessor may examine, when testing may occur, which methods are allowed, and what must remain untouched. Clear boundaries protect users and prevent scope disputes.

Practical steps

List exact domains, IP ranges, applications, cloud accounts, APIs, mobile builds, and environments. Identify excluded assets, third-party services, production constraints, allowed source addresses, time windows, and authorized test identities.

Scope and safety boundaries

Define prohibited actions such as denial-of-service, persistence, destructive changes, social engineering, or accessing real customer records unless a specific activity has been separately approved with safeguards.

Common mistakes to avoid

A domain's public availability does not establish permission. Shared infrastructure and outsourced services may need separate owner approval. Pause and contact the engagement lead when a target or impact is ambiguous.

How to verify improvement

Have the asset owner and tester approve the same version of the scope. Track changes, record approvals, and compare the final report to the original boundary list.
