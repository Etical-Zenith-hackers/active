Frequently asked questions

Can a confirmed blockchain transfer be reversed?

Usually not by the sender or a private investigator. A custodian, token issuer or authority may have limited options in particular circumstances, but availability depends on policy, law and facts. No one should promise that a confirmed transfer can routinely be cancelled.

Does a wallet address identify its owner?

No. An address is a public identifier for on-chain activity, not a verified real-world identity. It may be controlled by an individual, service, contract or multiple parties. Additional records and lawful procedures may be needed before attribution is made.

Should I share a recovery phrase with an investigator?

No. A seed phrase or private key gives control over wallet assets. Do not share it with an investigator, support agent, recovery company or website. A professional can usually begin with public addresses, transaction hashes and non-secret records.

What should I prepare for an initial review?

Prepare a concise timeline, public addresses, transaction hashes, asset and network details, amounts, timestamps, original communications and steps already taken. Keep passwords, private keys, seed phrases and authentication codes out of the evidence package.

Does tracing guarantee recovery?

No. Tracing documents visible activity and may identify service touchpoints. Recovery is separate and may require cooperation from a custodian, legal authority or another specific mechanism. A report should explain what is known, what remains uncertain and what options may be available.

Additional case-handling guidance

When more than one person reviews an incident, agree on a consistent naming and time convention. Give each item a reference number and use that reference in notes, reports and follow-up messages. Record whether an item is an original export, screenshot, working copy or analyst-created diagram. These controls reduce the chance that a later reviewer mistakes an illustrative image for a primary record.

Do not over-collect unrelated personal information. A wallet history may contain ordinary purchases, payroll activity, donations or transfers involving other people. Keep the investigation limited to the relevant time period and addresses where possible. If a broader review becomes necessary, document why the scope changed and who authorized the additional work. Store sensitive records in a restricted location and share only the material required for the stated purpose.

Be cautious when interpreting third-party analytics, public address labels and online reports. Such information may be useful, but its source, age and methodology matter. A label can change, an address can be shared by a service, and a transaction cluster may be based on a heuristic rather than direct proof. Describe the evidence supporting each conclusion and avoid attributing intent or identity beyond what the records establish.

Keep an accurate record of communications with a wallet provider, exchange, platform or authority. Include the date, official contact route, case number, documents submitted and any response. If a provider declines to act, record the stated reason rather than interpreting silence as confirmation of a theory. A transparent case file remains useful even when the outcome is uncertain or no assets are returned.

How to document uncertainty and next steps

A professional case note should explain both what the evidence supports and what it does not support. Use direct language such as “the explorer records a transfer from address A to address B” rather than assuming the transaction proves who operated either address. If a service label or analytical cluster is relevant, identify its source and date. This helps readers understand the difference between a public fact and an investigative lead.

Before sharing a report, check transaction identifiers, token amounts, time conversions and destination addresses against source records. Long identifiers can be copied incorrectly, and different applications may format token decimals or timestamps differently. A second-person check is useful for high-value entries. Preserve the first version of the evidence table and record later corrections instead of silently replacing earlier values.

Keep the scope proportionate and lawful. Do not attempt to access a suspected recipient's account, impersonate a service employee, threaten an address holder or bypass platform controls. Those actions can create additional risk and undermine a legitimate investigation. If information is held by an exchange or another custodian, use its official reporting channel and obtain legal advice about any formal request that may be needed.
