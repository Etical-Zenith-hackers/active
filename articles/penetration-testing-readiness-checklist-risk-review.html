Purpose and planning

A well-planned security assessment should answer a defined business question, use proportionate methods, and result in decisions that can be verified. Agree on outcomes before choosing tools or providers.

Practical checklist

Define business objectives and critical assets; inventory the environment; identify a decision-maker and technical contact; agree on authorized targets, dates, exclusions, test accounts, and emergency contacts; request clear deliverables and retest criteria.

Budget, scope, and trade-offs

Compare proposals on relevant experience, coverage, evidence quality, reporting, confidentiality, and remediation support—not only headline price. Separate essential scope from optional work and document assumptions that affect cost.

Common mistakes and boundaries

Avoid vague scope, guaranteed-security promises, unapproved production tests, or assuming third-party systems are included. Public accessibility does not establish permission; obtain written authorization from the responsible owner.

Measure the result

Check whether the final report addresses agreed objectives, ties findings to evidence, explains impact and limitations, and assigns actionable fixes. Track owners and deadlines, then retest important corrections.
