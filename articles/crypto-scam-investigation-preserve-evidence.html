Crypto Scam Investigation: Preserving Evidence Before It Disappears

By Zenith Hackers Intelligence

A practical guide to documenting cryptocurrency scams, preserving transaction evidence and creating a clear investigative timeline.

\\ ## Cryptocurrency scams leave evidence

Crypto scams can involve fake investment platforms, impersonation, fraudulent trading opportunities, romance scams, phishing and other forms of deception.

The first step after discovering a suspected scam is to preserve evidence.

Save transaction information

Record every relevant transaction hash, wallet address, blockchain network, amount and date.

Do not rely solely on screenshots.

Preserve communications

Keep emails, messaging conversations, invoices, websites, usernames, advertisements and payment instructions connected with the incident.

Where possible, preserve original copies.

Document the timeline

Create a chronological record showing when contact began, what was promised, when payments were made and when the problem was discovered.

A timeline can help investigators understand the sequence of events.

Report compromised accounts

If an exchange account, email account or wallet has been compromised, contact the relevant provider through its official support channel.

Change passwords and enable appropriate security controls.

Watch for recovery scams

After a crypto scam, victims may receive messages from supposed recovery agents.

Never provide seed phrases or private keys to someone claiming they need them to recover funds.

Blockchain tracing

Where cryptocurrency was transferred, blockchain analysis may help establish the movement of assets.

However, tracing does not guarantee recovery.

Final thoughts

Strong evidence preservation can make a legitimate investigation more useful and easier to verify.
