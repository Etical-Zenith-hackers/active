\\ ## Understanding blockchain forensics

Blockchain forensics is the process of examining blockchain records to understand how digital assets moved between addresses.

Unlike traditional financial systems, many public blockchains maintain transaction records that can be independently inspected.

Begin with known evidence

A blockchain investigation can begin with a wallet address, transaction hash, token contract, amount and approximate transaction date.

Accurate starting information reduces the possibility of following unrelated transactions.

Transaction graph analysis

Investigators can organize transactions into a chronological graph showing transfers between addresses.

This can help identify repeated movements, intermediate addresses and possible service-provider destinations.

Exchange and custodian involvement

If assets move to a centralized exchange or custodian, additional records may potentially exist.

Access to private account information should occur through the account holder, authorized representatives or appropriate legal processes.

Preserve the original evidence

Screenshots are useful, but transaction hashes and original records are particularly important.

Record the blockchain network, transaction ID, address and date for every relevant transaction.

Do not confuse tracing with identification

Blockchain analysis can show that assets moved from one address to another. It does not automatically prove who controls an address.

Attribution may require additional evidence.

Recovery considerations

Even when assets are successfully traced, recovery depends on circumstances outside the blockchain itself.

No legitimate investigator should guarantee that cryptocurrency will be recovered.

Final thoughts

Blockchain forensics can provide a transparent evidentiary foundation for legitimate cryptocurrency investigations.
